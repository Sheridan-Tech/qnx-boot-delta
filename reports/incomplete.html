What this report establishes

Boot Delta compares a documented subset of QNX 8 startup declarations. It does not execute commands, inspect target filesystems, compile an IFS image, or measure a QNX target.

  • Internal waits stop waiting when the path is observed or the configured timeout expires. A preceding wait is not proof of successful resource readiness.
  • Foreground/background changes and reordered declarations are review prompts. A foreground process may exit or daemonize; its lifetime is not inferred.
  • The wait total adds known built-in timeout declarations. Other commands, target scheduling, and excluded behavior are outside that sum.
  • Input gaps and ambiguous matching must be inspected. Zero findings is not a successful-boot or safety certification.
  • Optional contracts express a project's chosen configuration requirements. They do not add runtime enforcement.
  • High, medium, and low are review priorities, not proven faults or safety ratings.
  • The configured wait ceiling sums recognized built-in timeout settings, including the 5.0-second default. It is not measured boot time or a total boot-time bound.
  • A successful stat() does not establish that a service is fully ready. After a wait timeout, a dependency may still be unavailable.
  • External executable behavior, foreground process termination, and target hardware state are not established by this source comparison.
  • Edited occurrences of 'waitfor' cannot be matched uniquely (2 before, 1 after). They are shown as unmatched source removals/additions; no one-to-one replacement identity is asserted.
  • The wait inventory is incomplete for at least one input. Excluded entries and parser coverage gaps may hide additional configured waits; the numeric delta compares recognized subtotals only.
